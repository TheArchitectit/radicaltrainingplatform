# Manage Azure subscriptions and governance (S1.3)

*Module S1.3 — Azure Administrator AZ-104 study track. Independent study material — see the disclaimer at the end.*

Identity and access control individual actions. Governance controls whole populations at once — picture nested boxes with rules stapled to the outside. Policies, locks, tags, and budgets work the same way: attach something at a container, and everything inside is affected. Where you attach the rule is most of the skill.

## The container hierarchy

**Management groups** sit above subscriptions, so an organisation with many subscriptions can apply access and policy once instead of fifty times. They nest, and a subscription belongs to exactly one of them. Because assignments inherit downward, a policy assigned at a management group reaches every subscription beneath it.

**Subscriptions** are both a billing boundary and a security boundary; their value is separation — different payment arrangements, different environments, different blast radii. A subscription can be moved between management groups without being recreated.

**Resource groups** hold resources that share a lifecycle. Two rules matter: a resource lives in exactly one resource group, and resource groups do not nest. Deleting one deletes everything inside it — which is why they are usually organised by "things that get deleted together", such as one application's full stack.

## Resource locks

A **lock** is a blunt instrument attached to a resource, resource group, or subscription, and inherited by children. **CanNotDelete** allows reads and modifications but refuses deletion; **ReadOnly** freezes the resource so even settings changes are refused.

The key detail: locks apply to the **control plane**, not the data plane. A ReadOnly lock on a storage account stops you changing its configuration in the portal, but an application can still write blobs into it. If the symptom is "the portal refuses my change but the app works fine", think lock. Managing locks requires Owner or User Access Administrator; Contributor is not enough.

## Tags

**Tags** are name/value pairs for organising resources for billing and reporting — `costCenter: 4471`, `environment: production`. Two limitations cause most problems. Tags are **not inherited**: a tag on a resource group does not appear on the resources inside it, so cost reports grouped by tag show untagged resources unless something enforces the tag. And tag rules are per-resource, with a limit on how many one resource can carry. A policy that appends the tag is the usual fix.

## Azure Policy

**Azure Policy** evaluates resource properties and can refuse or correct them. A **definition** is one rule, such as "only these regions are allowed". An **initiative** is a named collection of definitions. An **assignment** attaches one to a scope, with parameters. An **effect** decides what happens when a resource does not comply.

Common effects: **Deny** blocks the request outright, **Audit** records non-compliance without blocking, and **Append** adds a property during creation. Deny acts on *new* requests, so assigning it does not fix what already exists — that is what a **remediation task** is for. An **exemption** carves a resource out of an assignment, usually with an expiry date, for a documented exception.

## Cost management and Azure Advisor

**Cost Management** provides cost analysis and **budgets**. A budget is a spending target with alert thresholds; alerts fire on actual or forecasted spend and can trigger an action group. A budget does not stop spending — it tells you; quotas and policy are what constrain.

**Azure Advisor** returns recommendations across cost, security, reliability, performance, and operational excellence, and is the fastest way to find idle or oversized resources.

## Worked example: three small decisions

1. **A developer cannot add a diagnostic setting to a storage account, but the application writes blobs normally.** The account carries a **ReadOnly** lock. Remove or downgrade it at the scope that holds it — it may be inherited from the resource group.
2. **A team must be prevented from deploying outside two regions, and 30 resources already sit in a third region.** Assign a policy with the **Deny** effect for new deployments, and run a remediation for what already exists. Deny alone will not move them.
3. **Finance wants a warning before the monthly bill crosses a threshold.** Create a **budget** on the subscription with an alert at the chosen percentage. Expect an alert, not an automatic shutdown.
