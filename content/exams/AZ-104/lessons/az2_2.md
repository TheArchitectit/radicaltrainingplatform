# Configure and manage storage accounts (S2.2)

*Module S2.2 — Azure Administrator AZ-104 study track. Independent study material — see the disclaimer at the end.*

Lesson 2.1 assumed a storage account existed. This one is about the account itself: decisions you make when you create it and cannot casually undo. Picture one account as a bundle of choices — what kind it is, how many copies of your data exist and where, and who holds the encryption key.

## Kinds, services, and performance tiers

A **general-purpose v2** account is the default and supports all four core services:

- **Blob** storage — unstructured objects, the subject of lesson 2.3.
- **Azure Files** — managed SMB and NFS file shares.
- **Queue** storage — small messages between application components.
- **Table** storage — a simple key-attribute store.

The **performance tier** is a separate decision. **Standard** accounts use disk-backed storage and suit most workloads. **Premium** accounts are SSD-backed and are created for a specific workload — premium block blobs, premium file shares, or premium page blobs for virtual machine disks — rather than for all services at once.

## Redundancy

Redundancy answers "how many copies, and how far apart". Read these as a ladder:

- **LRS (locally redundant)** keeps three copies within one datacenter. It survives disk and rack failure, not the loss of the datacenter.
- **ZRS (zone redundant)** keeps copies across three availability zones in one region. It survives the loss of an entire zone, and the endpoint does not change.
- **GRS (geo redundant)** is LRS in the primary region plus an asynchronous copy to a paired secondary region. It survives a regional failure, but the secondary copy is not readable while the primary is healthy, and moving to it is a deliberate step.
- **GZRS** is ZRS in the primary region plus the same asynchronous copy elsewhere: zone and regional resilience together.
- **RA-GRS** and **RA-GZRS** add read access to the secondary region's endpoint, for applications that must keep reading during a regional problem.

## Object replication

**Object replication** asynchronously copies blobs from a source container to a destination container, which may sit in a different account or region, and unlike the geo-redundant secondary copy the destination is readable. Both accounts must have **blob versioning** enabled — the setting people most often forget when replication silently does nothing.

## Encryption

All data written to Azure Storage is encrypted at rest with platform-managed keys, and this cannot be switched off. Two options go further:

- **Infrastructure encryption** applies a second, independent layer of encryption at the infrastructure level. It is chosen when the account is created and cannot be turned on later.
- **Customer-managed keys** move control of the key-encryption key into a key vault you own, so you can rotate or revoke it. The storage account needs a managed identity with access to that vault.

## Working with data: Storage Explorer and AzCopy

**Azure Storage Explorer** is a graphical desktop application for browsing accounts, containers, and shares, and it can connect using a key, a SAS, or an Entra ID sign-in. **AzCopy** is the command-line tool for bulk work — it is built for throughput and offers a `copy` operation for one-off transfers and a `sync` operation that makes a destination match a source.

## Worked example: choosing for a new workload

1. A reporting application in one region must keep serving reads if a single availability zone fails, with no change to its endpoint. LRS is out — it does not span zones. ZRS gives three zones behind one endpoint.
2. The same team also wants copies in a second region for a disaster, and is willing to accept that the copies are not readable until they act. GZRS adds that regional copy on top of zone resilience.
3. Compliance then asks that the encryption key be one the company controls and can revoke. Enable customer-managed keys in a key vault and grant the account's managed identity access — remembering that infrastructure encryption, if it is wanted too, had to be chosen at creation time.
