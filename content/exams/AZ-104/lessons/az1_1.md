# Manage Microsoft Entra users and groups (S1.1)

*Module S1.1 — Azure Administrator AZ-104 study track. Independent study material — see the disclaimer at the end.*

Everything an administrator does with people starts in one place: a **tenant**. A tenant is a dedicated instance of Microsoft Entra ID holding your users, groups, and app registrations, and it is the identity boundary for every subscription that trusts it. The tenant is the *who*; the subscription is the *what*.

## Tenants and the users inside them

A tenant is created when you sign up for Azure and arrives with a default domain name like `contoso.onmicrosoft.com`. You can add custom domains you own, so users sign in as `alex@contoso.com`.

Two **user types** exist:

- **Member** — an account that belongs to your tenant. You create it, you control its password, and it is deleted with the tenant.
- **Guest** — an external collaborator invited through B2B collaboration. They authenticate with credentials from their own home organisation, and your tenant holds a representation of them rather than their password. Guests appear in the directory and can join groups, but bring their own identity.

The practical difference is where the credential lives: if their password is managed by their own IT department, they are a guest in your tenant.

## Groups: type and membership

A group is the unit of scale. Granting access to a group rather than to twenty individuals means the next new hire is one membership away from productive, and one removal away from cut off.

There are two group **types**. **Security groups** are the general-purpose container for granting access to resources, apps, and policies. **Microsoft 365 groups** are collaboration containers that also provision a shared mailbox, calendar, and SharePoint site.

There are three **membership types**: **assigned** (you add members by hand), **dynamic user** (a rule on user attributes such as department or country keeps the list correct), and **dynamic device** (a rule on device attributes, available for security groups only).

Dynamic membership requires a paid Microsoft Entra ID P1 or P2 plan. It is also the answer to "the list keeps going stale" — a group maintained by hand will drift.

## Administrative units and licence assignment

An administrator role such as User Administrator normally applies to the whole tenant. An **administrative unit** narrows that: place a subset of users (or groups and devices) in the unit, then assign a role scoped to it. The helpdesk can reset passwords for one department without touching the rest of the directory.

**Licences** go to a user, or to a group so everyone in it receives one. Two things trip people up. Group-based licensing needs a paid Entra ID plan. And a user must have a **usage location** set before a geographically dependent licence can be assigned — a missing usage location is the classic reason an assignment fails.

## Guests and self-service password reset

**Self-service password reset (SSPR)** lets users change or reset their own password after registering authentication methods. You enable it for nobody, everybody, or a selected group — piloting with a group is the usual rollout. Writing a reset password back to an on-premises directory requires a paid plan and Microsoft Entra Connect. If the reset page does not recognise a user, check whether SSPR covers a group they are in and whether they registered a method.

## Worked example: three small decisions

1. **A new hire cannot be assigned a licence; the portal complains.** The account exists and is a member user. The likely cause is a missing **usage location** — set the country, then assign. A group or a guest conversion does not address the error.
2. **A contractor needs access to a resource group for six months.** Invite them as a **guest**. Their password stays with their employer, and access ends when you remove the guest or the assignment.
3. **The "All Marketing" group is wrong every month and someone fixes it by hand.** Recreate it as a security group with **dynamic user** membership on the department attribute — assuming the tenant has P1 or P2.
